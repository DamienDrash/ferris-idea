## Context

ADR-0002 (2026-10-30, GO-3) chose `MIT OR Apache-2.0`. Nothing was ever distributed under it: the
repository is private, has one rights holder, no forks, no releases and `publish = false` in every
crate (checked on 2026-11-14; whether a copy left the Project Owner's hands by another route is for
him to confirm, open question 8).

On 2026-11-14 the Project Owner set a new requirement: everyone may see and use the project, and
"kommerzielle Nutzung nur mit meiner Zustimmung". A licence with such a limit is not an open-source
licence (Open Source Definition, criterion 6), so the project is called **source-available**.

The requirement left one question open, because for an IDE it decides who can use it at all: does a
developer who uses Ferris IDEA at work need consent?

## Decision

1. **Owner's decision 1 (2026-11-14):** the project is offered so that everyone may see and use it;
   commercial use needs his consent.
2. **Owner's decision 2 (2026-11-14):** asked whether a developer may use Ferris IDEA for free at
   their job, he chose "Beruflich frei, kein Weiterverkauf": professional use is free; selling
   Ferris IDEA or building a competing offering on it needs his consent.
3. **Licence:** the **PolyForm Shield License 1.0.0, unmodified**, for the project's own code and
   documentation. `LICENSE.md` holds the text as published at
   <https://polyformproject.org/licenses/shield/1.0.0>, followed by the `Required Notice:` line
   the licence defines. That line is a placeholder until the Project Owner names the licensor
   (open question 1). Its one limit: "Any purpose is a permitted purpose, except for providing
   any product that competes with the software or any product the licensor or any of its
   affiliates provides using the software."
4. **Wording:** the project is "source-available". It is never described as open source, free
   software, OSI-approved or fair source.
5. **Third-party parts keep their licences.** The unchanged keymaps, themes and icons from
   `JetBrains/intellij-community` stay under Apache-2.0, JetBrains Mono under OFL-1.1, every
   dependency under its own licence. Apache-2.0 section 4 allows other terms for the work as a
   whole as long as its conditions are kept. Texts: `LICENSES/`, `NOTICE`,
   `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md` (generated by `scripts/third-party-notices.sh`).
6. **Cargo manifests.** PolyForm Shield has no identifier in the SPDX licence list (checked on
   2026-11-14 against list data of 2026-10-28, which has PolyForm Noncommercial and Small Business
   only), so the manifests use `LicenseRef-PolyForm-Shield-1.0.0`:
   - workspace and every crate without bundled third-party assets, and `fuzz/`:
     `LicenseRef-PolyForm-Shield-1.0.0`;
   - `crates/ui` (embeds the Apache-2.0 keymaps, themes and icons):
     `LicenseRef-PolyForm-Shield-1.0.0 AND Apache-2.0`;
   - `crates/workbench` (embeds JetBrains Mono): `LicenseRef-PolyForm-Shield-1.0.0 AND OFL-1.1`.

   `publish = false` stays everywhere. `deny.toml` skips the unpublished workspace crates
   (`[licenses.private]`); the project's licence is not added to the list of licences allowed for
   dependencies.
7. **Crates with ported Apache-2.0 code** (ADR-0006; none exists today) are
   `LicenseRef-PolyForm-Shield-1.0.0 AND Apache-2.0` at crate level instead of `Apache-2.0` only.
   The ported files keep the Apache header and stay under Apache-2.0.
8. **Public release:** a new repository with a fresh history; the working repository stays
   private. Its history and tags carry the former licence files and are not offered to anyone.

## Alternatives

| Licence | Use at work without consent | Why not |
|---|---|---|
| `MIT OR Apache-2.0` (ADR-0002) | yes | No limit on selling or competing at all. |
| PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0 | no | Matches "commercial use only with consent" literally, but every developer at a job, every freelancer and every company evaluation would need consent. The Project Owner decided against that (decision 2). |
| PolyForm Strict 1.0.0 | no | As Noncommercial, and no changes or redistribution. |
| PolyForm Small Business 1.0.0 | under a size threshold | A size limit instead of consent; not what was asked. |
| Functional Source License 1.1 | yes | The closest alternative: it also blocks competing use only, in other words. Each version becomes Apache-2.0 or MIT two years after its release. Not picked; the Project Owner chose the licence without a conversion date. |
| Business Source License 1.1 | unclear ("production use" undefined for an IDE) | Needs a self-written use grant; converts to an open-source licence after at most four years. |
| Elastic License 2.0 | yes | Written for server software: blocks hosted services and tampering with licence keys, which Ferris IDEA does not have (ADR-0007). |
| Apache-2.0 with Commons Clause | yes | Blocks selling only; vague ("substantially"); often mistaken for Apache-2.0. |
| CC BY-NC 4.0 | probably no | Creative Commons advises against its licences for software. |
| Own licence text | as written | Standard terms under German law (§§ 305 ff. BGB): unclear clauses are read against the author; no case law, no scanner support. |

## Consequences

- **What the licence does and does not do (reviewer's note, 2026-11-14).** It restricts
  competing products, not charging as such: it forbids providing a product that competes with
  Ferris IDEA or with another product the licensor provides using it, "even when provided free of
  charge". It does not forbid earning money: a company may use Ferris IDEA unpaid for ever, a
  product that does not compete may contain parts of it, and passing on unchanged copies for a
  fee or selling support for Ferris IDEA is not clearly excluded. Whether that matches "kein
  Weiterverkauf" is an open decision for the Project Owner with a lawyer (open question 2).
  `LICENSING.md` therefore speaks only in the licence's terms and does not use the word resale.
- **The legal basis is older than this decision.** The project's internal legal analysis and the
  legal advice of 2026-10-30 it reports assume a private, non-commercial project under
  `MIT OR Apache-2.0`. They predate the change of licence, the offer of separate licences by the
  licensor, and the decision to prepare a publication. They do not cover either (open question 3).
- **No technical enforcement.** No licence keys, checks or telemetry (ADR-0007 stands).
- **Contributions need an inbound grant.** To keep granting other licences (commercial licences, a
  later change of licence) the Project Owner needs rights in every contribution that go beyond
  the public licence. Without them he would hold a contribution under PolyForm Shield only, like
  anyone else. `CONTRIBUTING.md` still states the former dual licence and must change before
  outside contributions are accepted. Proposed sentence for it (a proposal; the wording needs a
  lawyer, open question 7):

  > By submitting a contribution you keep your copyright and grant the project owner a
  > non-exclusive, worldwide, perpetual, irrevocable, royalty-free licence to use, reproduce,
  > modify, distribute and sublicense your contribution, including the right to license it under
  > the project's public licence and under other terms, and a patent licence for it to the same
  > extent; you confirm that you have the right to grant this, and you sign off each commit under
  > the Developer Certificate of Origin.

  Until a contributor agreement in this sense exists, code contributions from others cannot be
  merged; issues and discussion can be taken.
- **Brand assets.** `docs/design/BRAND_GUIDELINES.md` §4 says the symbol, wordmark and app icon
  ship "under its licences (MIT OR Apache-2.0)" (released 2026-11-07). `TRADEMARKS.md` proposes
  instead: not under the code licence, all rights reserved. *Decided 2026-11-16:* the Project
  Owner withdrew the release of 2026-11-07 before any publication; `TRADEMARKS.md` applies alone
  and §4 of the guidelines says so. The guidelines also still carry the GitHub topic `open-source` and the word "open-source-oriented".
- **Documents that still state the former licence** and are owned by other work: `README.md`
  (badge, licence section with links to the removed `LICENSE-MIT` and `LICENSE-APACHE`),
  `CONTRIBUTING.md`, `docs/design/BRAND_GUIDELINES.md`, rule 2 of `CLAUDE.md`/`AGENTS.md`
  ("Apache-2.0 crate"), GO-3 in `docs/legal/RESEARCH_BOUNDARIES.md` and the internal status file,
  and the SPDX lines of the Java sources and the manifest in `crates/languages/java/debug-private/`
  (changing the manifest changes the committed jar and its recorded hash). *Update 2026-11-15:* the
  Java sources, the manifest and the jar name the PolyForm Shield licence, and GO-3 records the
  change.
- **Notices are release work.** `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md` is regenerated when `Cargo.lock` changes
  (`scripts/third-party-notices.sh --check`) and ships inside every binary package; an entry in
  the About dialog and an MCP tool for it (ADR-0008) are still to build.
- **Dependency policy unchanged.** Permissive licences only (`deny.toml`); no dependency licence
  conflicts with PolyForm Shield. MPL-2.0 packages stay under MPL-2.0 file by file.

## Open questions before publication

For a lawyer unless marked otherwise.

1. **The licensor is not named.** `LICENSE.md` and `NOTICE` carry the placeholder
   `Required Notice: Copyright 2026 <legal name of the licensor — to be supplied by the project
   owner>`. The licence defines the licensor as "the individual or entity offering these terms";
   only the licensor can consent and enforce, so the line must name a legal person or entity
   (with the project URL, if wanted) before anything is published. The contact for licence
   requests in `LICENSING.md` is a placeholder too. No file in the repository names a licensor.
   (Project Owner; lawyer: whether a pseudonymous licensor is workable in Germany, and provider
   identification if licences are sold.)
2. **Fit of licence and intent: competing products, not payment.** Does PolyForm Shield cover
   everything the Project Owner means by "kein Weiterverkauf" and "commercial use only with my
   consent"? It restricts providing competing products, not charging as such: passing on
   unchanged copies for a fee, paid support or installation, or selling a product that contains
   parts of Ferris IDEA without competing, is not clearly excluded. Also unsettled in the text: where
   passing on a copy ends and providing a competing product begins, and using Ferris IDEA as the
   tool to build a competitor (`LICENSING.md` states the project's reading, unchecked). If
   payment matters, the licence is the wrong one or needs a companion text. (Project Owner and
   lawyer.)
3. **The existing legal analysis and advice predate this decision.** The internal legal analysis
   recommends a lawyer's check before any publication and rests on a private, non-commercial
   project under `MIT OR Apache-2.0`; the advice of 2026-10-30 is older than the licence change,
   the offer of separate licences and the decision to publish (and than the agent-operated
   observation tools added on 2026-11-05 and 2026-11-07). A non-compete licence with licences on
   request moves the project towards commercial activity; the check has to cover that.
4. **Enforceability in Germany.** Public licences are standard terms (§§ 305 ff. BGB); doubts go
   against the licensor (§ 305c Abs. 2). Is "competes" certain enough, is the licence validly
   agreed by download (English text, consumers), and is the limit a condition of the grant, so
   that a breach is copyright infringement (§§ 69c, 97 UrhG), or a breach of contract only? How
   does the 32-day cure clause sit with German termination rules?
5. **Liability and warranty.** The exclusion applies "as far as the law allows". Liability for
   intent cannot be excluded (§ 276 Abs. 3 BGB), nor, in standard terms, for gross negligence or
   injury to life, body and health (§ 309 Nr. 7 BGB). Do the reduced standards for gratuitous
   grants (§§ 521, 523, 524, 599, 600 BGB by analogy) still apply when the free licence stands
   beside paid ones? Paid licences need their own contract.
6. **AI-generated code.** Copyright needs a human's own intellectual creation (§ 2 Abs. 2, § 69a
   Abs. 3 UrhG). Large parts of the code were written by AI agents under the Project Owner's
   specifications and direction. Where there is no copyright, a copyright licence restricts
   nothing. How far do specification, architecture, selection and review make him the author; is
   the work protected as a whole; does the licence bind as a contract; do the AI provider's terms
   give him the rights in the output; should `Co-Authored-By` trailers appear in a public history?
7. **Contributor agreement under German law:** form, § 31a UrhG (unknown types of use), §§ 32, 32a
   (remuneration), minors, employees contributing in working time (§ 69b UrhG).
8. **Earlier distribution.** The Project Owner confirms that no source or build left his hands
   under `MIT OR Apache-2.0`. With a fresh public history, the old history is not offered; does
   anything else need saying?
9. **Apache-2.0 material in own files** ("Logik-Vorlage" in `PROVENANCE.md`: action titles,
   identifiers, value tables, orderings): is the record in `NOTICE` and `PROVENANCE.md` enough, or
   do such files need the Apache header and a "modified" notice?
10. **Third-party logos in file-type icons** from the open platform (Docker, Kotlin, Python and
    others): the copyright licence does not cover those marks; relevant once licences are sold.
11. **Name and trademark.** "Ferris IDEA" is not registered as far as recorded. Clearance and
    registration (DPMA or EUIPO) before publication; distance to "ArtCraft" and to JetBrains'
    names (internal legal analysis, §7). Brand assets: all rights reserved, as `TRADEMARKS.md` proposes?
    Copyright in AI-drawn marks is uncertain; a registered mark does not depend on it.
12. **EU product rules once money is involved:** Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847)
    and Product Liability Directive (EU) 2024/2853 exempt free and open-source software supplied
    outside a commercial activity; a source-available product with paid licences probably does not
    qualify. Dates and scope not checked against the texts.
13. **Selling licences as a private person in Germany:** trade registration, VAT, provider
    identification on the project page, consumer rules for digital products (§§ 327 ff. BGB).
14. **EPL-licensed interfaces of the Java debug bundle** (`PROVENANCE.md`, the row of
    `crates/languages/java/debug-private/`): the reasoning there, once licences are sold.
15. **Native libraries.** libgit2 (GPL-2.0 with a linking exception) reaches the build through the
    UI toolkit; whether it is in each shipped binary, and DuckDB's own bundled third-party code,
    are to be confirmed per release when the notices are generated per platform.
